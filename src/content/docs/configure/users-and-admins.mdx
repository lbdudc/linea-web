**Configuration is for administrators.** They set the instance up; everyone else uses it. This follows the model of tools like n8n: an owner and admins configure, the rest work.

## What only administrators can do

| Area | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Git hosts** | Add, switch off or change a host |
| **Plugins** | Install, enable, disable, configure, download the config |
| **Users** | Create accounts, set passwords, grant or remove the administrator role |
| **Instance settings** | Session length, lockout rules, publishing rights, audit retention |

Everyone else gets `403` from these endpoints, and the interface hides the entries: there is no menu item, and the page address sends them home. What the page needs before anyone signs in (`GET /api/users/config`, `GET /api/plugins/enabled`) stays public and carries no settings.

## Choosing the first administrators

`ADMIN_USERS` is a comma-separated list of **git usernames** who are administrators from the start:

```ini
ADMIN_USERS=alice,bob
```

<Aside type="tip" title="On GitLab or GitHub">
Use the **GitLab or GitHub username** exactly as it appears on the host. They are administrators the first time they sign in.
</Aside>

<Aside type="tip" title="On the local host">
Accounts do not exist yet, so LINEA creates them at start with the password in `LINEA_ADMIN_PASSWORD`. Change it after the first sign-in.
</Aside>

After that, administrators manage the role in the interface:

<Steps>

1. Open **Configuration → Users**.
2. Pick a person and switch **Administrator** on or off.
3. The **last administrator cannot be removed**, so you can never lock yourself out.

</Steps>

## Project roles are separate

Being an administrator does not give rights on repositories. What someone can do on a line or a product comes from their role **on the git host**:

| Action | Role needed |
|---|---|
| See a line and its products | Guest or higher |
| Create a product | Reporter on the line |
| Generate a product, retry jobs | Maintainer on the product |

On the `local` host the same levels apply to members of the line.

## Service accounts

Services that act on behalf of people, such as the AI agents service, authenticate with a **service key** instead of a password. LINEA lets the service do what the person it acts for could do, and logs both.

```ini
LINEA_SERVICE_KEYS=agents-plugin:<secret>
```

The service sends `Authorization: Service <key>` and `X-Act-As: <user>`. See [Plugins](/configure/plugins/).

## Audit

LINEA records administrative actions such as publishing a package. Entries older than `auditRetentionDays` (180 by default) are removed. See [Instance settings](/configure/instance-settings/).
